ROTARY CODE OF  CONDUCT
As a Rotarian, I will:
1. Act with integrity and high ethical  standards in my personal and 
professional  life
2. Deal fairly with others and treat them and  their occupations with respect
3. Use my professional skills through Rotary  to mentor young people, help 
those with  special needs, and improve people’s  quality of life in my 
community and in the  world
4. Avoid behavior that reflects adversely on
5. Rotary or other Rotarians
6. Not seek special business or professional  advantages from other 
Rotarians

CLUB DIRECTORY YEAR 2025/26

Good Day My Fellow Rotarians, 
It is indeed a great honour and privilege to address our distinguished members in 
this Directory as we embark on another meaningful Rotary Year — one guided by 
our timeless motto, Service Above Self. 
Being a member of the Rotary Club of Kuala Lumpur Diraja since 2010, is not 
merely a designation but it is a commitment to a noble cause, a fellowship built on 
compassion and a lifelong journey of giving. Each of us may have joined Rotary 
for different reasons i.e: to serve our community, to contribute to humanitarian 
causes, or to find fellowship among kindred spirits but yet we are all bound by one 
shared purpose that is to make a positive difference in the lives of others. 
In Rotary, we abide by THE 4-WAY TEST and discover the joy of service, the strength of friendship and the 
fulfilment that comes from helping those in need. We enjoy every moment in Rotary — not only through the 
bonds we build but through the impact we create together.As Rotarians, our mission is deeply anchored in our 
5 core values — Service, Fellowship, Diversity, Integrity, and Leadership.These values define who we are 
and how we act.They remind us that service is our highest calling along with fellowship is our strength and the 
diversity enriches our unity, integrity sustains our trust and leadership empowers us to inspire change. 
Our community projects remain at the very heart of Rotary. Whether it is empowering youth, advancing 
education, supporting healthcare, protecting the environment or uplifting the underprivileged — every act of 
service we render echoes Rotary’s global vision of creating hope and transforming lives.As we move forward, 
let us continue to be guided by Rotary’s 7 Avenues of Service:Club Service/Vocational Service/Community 
Service/International Service/Youth Service/Public Image/Environment Service. Let us also remain 
steadfast in our commitment to support and strengthen our club.Our focus must be on nurturing membership, 
fostering fellowship, and developing future leaders who uphold Rotary’s principles. The strength of our club lies 
not in numbers but in the dedication, unity and shared vision of its members. 
Equally important is our mission to enhance Rotary’s public image and awareness. By showcasing our initiatives 
and sharing our impact stories, we can inspire others to join our cause and contribute to a better, more 
compassionate world. 
Fellow Rotarians, as we commence Rotary Year 2025/2026, let us renew our commitment with enthusiasm, 
humility and joy. Let us embrace every opportunity to serve, to lead, and to uplift. Let us continue to embody 
the true essence of Rotary — where Service Above Self is not just our motto but a way of life.Together, let 
us make this year a remarkable one — a year filled with meaningful service, lasting fellowship, and collective 
achievements that will continue to inspire generations to come. Rotary continues to shine as a beacon of hope, 
humanity, and harmony in our world. 
By the way, please do join our Presidents/Club’s Project Blue Dot and we need your 100% support. 
As Helen Keller so beautifully reminded us, 
“Alone we can do so little; together we can do so much.” 
Yours in Service 
President Rtn Dato Dr Prakash Rao Krishnan

THE ROTARY CLUB OF KUALA LUMPUR DIRAJA
A Brief History
The Rotary Club of Kuala Lumpur existed in 
spirit as early as 1918  when leaders of govern-
ment, commerce and professions residing in  
Kuala Lumpur mooted the idea of forming an 
association to promote  community service and 
inter-community harmony.
This lofty idea gained tremendous support from 
leaders of the various  communities in Kuala 
Lumpur and the Concord Club, organised on  
Rotary lines, was formed in the early 1920s.
In 1928, through the visit of Rtn James W Da -
vidson, Rotary  International’s “Special Com -
missioner”, the Concord Club evolved  into the 
Provisional Rotary Club of Kuala Lumpur on 
20th July 1928.
The Rotary Club of Kuala Lumpur was inaugu -
rated on 27th September  1929, with Andrew 
Caldecott (later Sir Andrew) as the Founder  
President, David Gammans (later Sir David) 
as the fi rst Secretary and  29 other Founder 
members, among whom were famous names 
such  as Raja Tun Uda, Choo Kia Peng (the first 
Vice President), Loke  Choe Thye, Mungo Park, A 
Viswalingam, Dato’ Hamsah Abdullah, Pat  Ben-
jamin Tallala, Khoo Teik Ee, Robert Boyd and A. 
Savage Bailey.
Through an unfortunate administrative mix-up, 
the Rotary Club of  Kuala Lumpur was formal -
ly chartered rather belatedly on 15th January  
1930, vide Charter No. 3268. By the time the 
Charter was received  membership in the club 
membership had increased to 65 and amongst  
them was Past District Governor Tan Sri Dato’ 
Gunn Lay Teik.
The club did not function during the Japanese 
Occupation, 1942-1946.  The fi rst postwar meet-
ing was convened in October 1946, with 33  
members. This membership grew to 65 by 1947.
The Rotary Club of Kuala Lumpur has had the 
honour of sponsoring  several Rotary, Rota -
ract and Interact Clubs. The ten Rotary Clubs it  
sponsored are Klang Coast (now Klang, 1930), 
Kuantan (1952/3),  Petaling Jaya (1960/1), Pudu 
(1963/4), Cameron Highlands (Defunct)  (1968/9), 
Gombak (1970/1), Damansara (1980/1), Kua -
la Lumpur West  (1989/90), Kampung Baharu 
(1996/97) and Putrajaya (2002).
The Interact Clubs sponsored are St. John’s In-
stitution (1962/3), SMK  Maxwell 1964/5), SMK 
Seri Bintang (1971/2), SMK Convent Bukit  Nanas 
(1972/3), SMK Tinggi Setapak, Sekolah Method -
ist Wesley  (Brickfields), SMK Aminuddin Baki, 
SMK Convent Jalan Peel, Sekolah  Sri Garden, 
Sekolah Alam Shah (since handed over to Pudu 
Rotary  Club) and Sekolah Harapan (now de -
funct).
Three Rotaract Clubs were sponsored, namely, 
Rotaract Club of Kuala  Lumpur (1967/8), Rot -
aract Club of University of Malaya (1971/2) and  
Rotaract Club of Stamford Secretarial Centre 
(1977/8), which later  became the Rotaract Club 
of Wanita Wilayah. It ceased operations  in 1986. 
Three Youthact Clubs were sponsored, namely 
Sri Garden  School (2001) and SMK Taman Desa 
(2002) and Alice Smith School  (2005).
A Rotary Community Corps (RCC) was estab -
lished iin RY 2002//03  at Kampung Sungei Lui 
and is still operating today.
Construction of the RM 6.5 mill BRDB Rotary 
Children’s Residence,  the club’ssignature proj-
ect, commenced in 2012 and was completed  in 
2015. It currently provides accommodation and 
services for 69 as directed by the welfare de -
partment children but will eventually house 100.

1. BACK TO SCHOOL: FREE SOCKS FOR B40
Together with Masmos Bhd, whom donated 
530,000 pairs of socks which are worth a 
market value of over RM1,600,000 our Rotary 
club worked with about 20 clubs in the District 
to distribute school socks for B40 kids
 
2. GIVE THEM A MILLION REASONS TO SMILE
With BRDB Bhd, a fundraising dinner was held 
that raised RM1,000,000 for the 70 children 
who were living at the BRDB -Rotary Children’s 
Residence in Seri Kembangan. The generous 
donations were timely in view of the long COVID 
shutdown prior to this when fundraising was 
extremely challenging.
3. LIGHT UP THEIR HOME
For Deepavali, members of our club got 
together and raised funds to rebuild the toilets 
and cupboards at an orphanage in Melawati, KL. 
The orphanage had about 30 children resident.
4. KARANGKRAF BOOK PURCHASE
Our club has a Rotary Community Corp in Sg. 
Lui, Selangor where we maintain a library 
for the benefit of the children there. For this 
project a number of Rotarians joined together 
under the leadership of Past District Governor 
Dato Haji Mustapha Ma who has had a long 
service record in education, to buy books from 
Karangkraf and deliver to Sg. Lui.
5. UKMSC GAMMA KNIFE CANCER TREATMENT
The club and the Universiti Kebangsaan 
Malaysia Specialist Centre agreed to cooperate 
on discounted pricing for B40 patients who 
qualify for a gamma knife brain cancer 
treatment. Brain cancer operations are highly 
intrusive but using gamma knife methodology, 
patients recovery rates are much faster than in 
conventional methods.
6. INSTALLATION AND URBAN FARMS
As part of the club project for the year, 
RM100,000 was raised in profits which were 
directed to the purchase of solar-powered 
organic urban farms for the Spastic Childrens’ 
Association of Selangor and Federal Territories 
and also ShelterHome PJ. The purpose of the 
units is to provide organic food to the residents 
there and to involve children to learn how to 
farm.
7. 3H AND SVC FOR REFUGEES
A group of UNHCS-accredited refugees mainly 
from Pakistan, Afghanistan and Palestine were 
screened in Batu Caves for high blood sugar, 
high cholesterol and high blood pressure. Many 
cases of first-time checks revealed several 
problems that are easily treatable with the 
proper medication. Besides, their vision was 
also tested under the Sayangi-mata Vision 
Clinic and several cases were identified for 
visual treatment. All staff at this event were 
club members and doctors from our club
8. FLOOD VICTIMS IN JOHOR
This year, major floods hit the West coast of 
Malaysia especially in Southern Johor so club 
members together with our Kuala Lumpur 
Rotary Charity Foundation donated money to 
selected families affected, identified by our 
fellow Rotary clubs in Johor.
9. HELP UNIVERSITY BURSARY
An agreement was reached with HELP 
University where our club would identify 
deserving students from the B40 community 
who can qualify for a course at HELP but cannot 
afford to pay for their studies. HELP generously 
allocated RM1,000,000 a year, every year, for five 
years.
10. ROTARY-OASIS EYE FUND
Oasis Eye Specialists approached our Club to 
set up a fund where customers of Oasis who 
are happy with their operation results and wish 
to thank Oasis may instead make a donation 
to our Foundation which will be separately 
identified and managed for eye operations. The 
initial seed donation was RM5,000.

THE OBJECT OF ROTARY
The Objectof ROTARY is to encourageand foster  the ideal of service as a basis
of worthy enterpriseand,in particular, to encourage  and foster:
1. The development of acquaintance as an
opportunityfor service;
2. High ethical standards in business and professions,the recognition of the
worthiness of all useful occupations, and the dignifying of each Rotarian’s
occupationas an opportunity to serve society;
3. The application of the ideal of service in each Rotarian’s personal,
business, and community life;
4. The advancement of international understanding, goodwil., and peace
through a world fellowship of business and professional persons united in
the ideal of service.
